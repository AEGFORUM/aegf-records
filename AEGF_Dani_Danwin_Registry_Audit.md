# AEGF Registry Audit — Findings & Proposed Update Process

**Contributor:** Dani Danwin
**Role:** Registry Steward, AI Execution Governance Forum
**Date added to record:** July 2026
**Status:** Founding Member / Steering Committee contribution — attributed in full, published with explicit written approval
**Disposition:** Adopted as operational process, implemented directly by the Registry Steward under standing mandate (no Steering Committee vote required — process change does not touch the 75% supermajority rule or member rights)

---

## 1. Summary

The Registry is well-populated and internally consistent at the level of member count: "All Members (26)" and "Steering Committee (11)" both check out against the actual listings. The core issues are not about missing people — they're about **missing metadata and missing process visibility**. Below are the specific findings, followed by a proposed submit → verify → publish process to close these gaps going forward.

---

## 2. Findings

### 2.1 Incomplete entry
- **Haseeb** (Founding Member) is the only listing across all 26 entries with no organisation/company field populated. Every other entry lists a company, protocol name, or "Independent."

### 2.2 No visible verification mechanism
- The Registry states: *"Every listing permanently attributed to its source"* and *"100% Attribution Guarantee — no output carrying a member's name goes live without their explicit written approval."*
- No entry shows any trace of that approval (no link, timestamp, or verification badge). The guarantee is asserted institutionally but not evidenced per-listing — worth noting given the Registry's own subject matter is execution evidence.

### 2.3 No dates on entries
- No listing shows when it was added or last updated. This makes it impossible to audit currency — e.g., whether a listing reflects a member's current work or something stated a year ago.

### 2.4 "Interim" status undefined
- Two Steering Committee members (Frank Schouten, Arvind SR) carry the label **"Interim Steering Committee"** alongside their primary role. Nowhere on the site is "interim" defined — how long it lasts, what triggers confirmation to full status, or who decides.

### 2.5 Dual listing without clarified relationship
- **Moad Abu-Alian, PhD, PMP** appears twice: once as "Enterprise Policy Architecture Advisor" in the main Registry, and separately as an "Advisor — Enterprise Implementation" in the Observers/Advisors/Pipeline section. It's unclear whether this is one role described two ways, or two distinct capacities that should be visually linked.

### 2.6 Quotes section doesn't distinguish committee membership
- The "No single person holds authority over another" quotes section mixes Steering Committee members (e.g., Dani Danwin, Frank Schouten) with non-Steering contributors (e.g., Tim Zlomke, Saad Muzafar) without any visual distinction. A reader landing on that page alone can't tell who is formally on the Steering Committee.

### 2.7 No public taxonomy guide for the Governance Transition Chain tags
- Every entry self-selects a combination of Evidence / Trust / Authority / Permission / Action / Consequence to describe its scope. There's no published guide on how many stages a listing may claim, or what evidence is expected to support a claimed stage. As the Registry grows, this risks tag inflation (members claiming broader scope than their work supports) with no check in place.

### 2.8 Empty categories without a status note
- "Observers" and "Pipeline" sections are currently empty; "Advisors" has one entry. This is likely just early-stage, but nothing on the page confirms these are active/monitored categories versus abandoned ones.

## 3. Proposed Update Process (Submit → Verify → Publish)

### Step 1 — Submit
- New listing requests go through a single intake form (or GitHub issue template) capturing: name, organisation, role/title, one-line description, Governance Transition Chain tag(s) claimed, and a link to supporting public work (site, repo, paper, or prior session contribution).
- Submitter explicitly confirms in writing that the listing text is accurate and may be published under the Attribution Guarantee.

### Step 2 — Verify
- Registry Steward checks:
  - Organisation/role fields are complete (closes finding 2.1).
  - Claimed chain tags are supported by the linked public work — not aspirational (addresses finding 2.7).
  - No duplicate identity conflicts with an existing listing (addresses finding 2.5 going forward).
- Verification outcome and date are logged internally (even if not shown publicly, this creates the audit trail the Registry currently lacks — finding 2.3).

### Step 3 — Publish
- Approved listing goes live with a **visible "Added/Updated" date** on the entry itself (closes 2.3).
- Steering Committee / Interim / Advisor status is shown as a **distinct visual tag**, separate from the individual's functional role, and consistent across every page that surfaces member info — Registry, Ecosystem Map, and the quotes section (closes 2.4 and 2.6).
- "Interim" status carries a defined review point (reviewed at the next Assembly/Session) rather than remaining open-ended.

### Step 4 — Ongoing integrity
- Annual (or per-Assembly) re-confirmation pass: Steward reaches out to all listed members to confirm the listing still accurately reflects their work, refreshing the "last updated" date whether or not content changes.
