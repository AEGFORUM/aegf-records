## The Question

> **"What must be governed before execution — rather than only audited afterward?"**

This is the question the founding circle identified as the intellectual anchor of the Forum.

It is not a simple question. It has no single answer. That is precisely why it belongs here.

---

## Why This Question

Most governance frameworks operate after the fact. They audit. They log. They report. They assign accountability once a consequence has already formed.

The emerging field represented by this circle is asking something different: what conditions must be verified, enforced, or established *before* an autonomous system is permitted to act — at the moment consequence is forming, not after it has formed.

That distinction — governance *before* execution versus governance *after the fact* — is the intellectual territory AEGF exists to map.

## The Governance Transition Chain

To give the circle a shared vocabulary — without imposing a shared framework — the following chain, developed by founding member **Tim Zlomke**, frames the session:

```
EVIDENCE → TRUST → AUTHORITY → PERMISSION → ACTION → CONSEQUENCE
```

Each transition in this chain represents a governance moment. The session question asks: which of these transitions is currently least governed? Most consequential? And what does governing it actually look like in practice?

You are not required to use this framework. It is offered as orientation, not constraint.

## How to Contribute

Your contribution is yours. AEGF does not edit, summarise, or reframe what you submit. It goes into the permanent record in your words, attributed to your name and project.

**To contribute, send your response directly to Raheem Larry Babatunde.**

Your submission should address the session question in whatever way is most natural for your work. There is no required format. There is no minimum or maximum length. A paragraph is enough. A structured analysis is equally welcome.

Suggested angles — use any, all, or none:

→ What does your work govern before execution — and why that moment specifically?

→ Which transition in the chain (Evidence → Trust → Authority → Permission → Action → Consequence) does your work address? Why is that transition the critical one?

→ What are you *not* governing yet — and what would it take to govern it?

→ What does "governed before execution" look like from your discipline, industry, or regulatory context?

→ Where do you see the most dangerous gap — the transition least governed today?
