# AEGF Ecosystem Report Vol. 1 — Domain-Lead Chapter Schema

**For:** All Domain Lead / Steering Committee contributors submitting a chapter for Vol. 1
**Format:** Markdown
**Length:** 800–1,500 words (flexible; contact Convener if your domain genuinely needs more)

---

## Required sections

1. **Title** — the governance question or boundary your chapter addresses.
2. **Contributor** — name, role, organization.
3. **Core Position** — your central claim, stated plainly, in 2–3 sentences.
4. **The Governance Problem** — what breaks, or goes unaddressed, without the perspective your chapter brings.
5. **Transition Chain Position** — where your work sits on the Governance Transition Chain (Evidence → Trust → Authority → Permission → Action → Consequence).
6. **What Must Happen Before Execution** — specific, practical conditions, from your domain's perspective.
7. **What Should Remain Auditable Afterward** — what a reviewer, regulator, or enterprise buyer should be able to verify after the fact.
8. **Open Question** — what your own work has not yet resolved.

## Optional section

**Evidentiary Status** — if you choose to characterize claims by evidentiary strength (e.g., Verified / Demonstrated / In-Progress / Unproven), that convention is welcome and consistent with AEGF's rigor standard. This is entirely your choice as the contributor, at whatever level of detail you're comfortable making public. AEGF does not require internal engineering detail, security posture, unresolved vulnerabilities, or proprietary implementation specifics — only what you determine is safe and appropriate for a permanent public record.

## Non-negotiables (per AEGF's standing principles)

- Written in your own words, attributed to you.
- No claim beyond what you can stand behind publicly and permanently.
- No editorial reframing by AEGF — your independent judgment stands as submitted, subject only to the Technical Rigor Checklist (claim traceability, scope boundaries, no silent contradiction, versioning).
- Nothing published without your explicit written approval of the final text.
- Vendor-agnostic framing is encouraged where the underlying governance problem is industry-wide, but not required — attribute what's yours to attribute.
