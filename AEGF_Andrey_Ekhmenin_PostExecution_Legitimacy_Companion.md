# Post-Execution Legitimacy in the AEGF Governance Transition Chain

**Contributor:** Andrey Ekhmenin
**Role:** Post-Execution Legitimacy Lead, AI Execution Governance Forum
**Date added to record:** July 2026
**Type:** AEGF companion note, bridging external research to the Forum's Governance Transition Chain
**Status:** Founding Member contribution — attributed in full, published with explicit written approval

---

Within the AEGF Governance Transition Chain, post-execution legitimacy addresses the question that arises after an AI system, agent, or governed process has acted: whether the authority, mandate, and governance basis that justified the execution remain identifiable and attributable after the resulting transition. Pre-execution governance determines whether an action may proceed; execution governance constrains how it proceeds; post-execution legitimacy examines whether the resulting governance state still preserves a demonstrable relationship to the basis from which the authority to act was derived.

This distinction is important because operational continuity does not by itself establish legitimacy continuity. An AI system, agent network, or institutional process may continue to operate, produce outputs, and remain procedurally compliant while the connection between its present authority and its original mandate becomes weakened, transformed, or unclear. The Origin property provides the analytical reference for examining that relationship. Origin is treated not as a separate governance layer or function, but as a property of Governance State through which the continuity of authority can be assessed across execution, transition, adaptation, and institutional change.

Within the Forum, the Post-Execution Legitimacy Lead role focuses on making this dimension visible in the governance architecture. The role does not validate legal acts, create authority, determine constitutional or regulatory validity, or replace compliance, assurance, audit, accountability, or technical evaluation. Its function is to identify where a completed execution or transition requires an additional legitimacy inquiry: whether the resulting Governance State remains demonstrably connected to the mandate, institutional basis, or source of authority under which the execution was initiated.
