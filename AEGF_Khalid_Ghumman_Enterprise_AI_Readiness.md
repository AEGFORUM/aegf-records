## 1. Executive Context

This working reference uses a fictional Oil & Gas Enterprise to illustrate the most common Enterprise AI and Data governance gaps encountered during the early stages of AI adoption. The organization operates Oracle ERP (Finance, HR, Procurement), IBM Maximo, multiple custom applications, Oracle and Microsoft SQL Server and some open-source databases, an Enterprise Data Warehouse, and a wide variety of structured, semi-structured, and unstructured operational information.

## 2. Key Observation

Enterprise AI does not start with an AI model.

It starts with trusted, governed, and use-case-ready data. Organizations that invest first in business-aligned data foundations are significantly better positioned to scale AI safely, responsibly, and successfully.

*This working reference reflects common enterprise AI and data governance patterns observed through professional experience and industry observations. It is intended as a practical discussion aid and starting point rather than a comprehensive audit or maturity assessment methodology.*

## 3. Biggest Enterprise AI & Data Governance Gaps

| Gap | Why it Matters | Priority | Recommended First Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI Strategy & Business Vision | No clear business outcomes, sponsorship or success measures. | Critical | Establish an AI Steering Committee and define 3–5 measurable business use cases. |
| Prioritized AI Use Cases | AI initiatives are technology-led instead of value-led. | Critical | Develop a prioritized AI use-case roadmap based on business value and feasibility. |
| Enterprise Data Inventory & Discovery | Data is fragmented across ERP, Maximo, custom apps and documents. | Critical | Create an enterprise data inventory with owners, sources and consumers. |
| Use-Case Data Readiness | Data is not prepared for specific AI use cases. | High | Assess data quality, completeness, metadata and accessibility for each priority use case. |
| Data Quality, Metadata & Lineage | Limited trust and explainability of enterprise data. | High | Profile critical datasets and implement metadata, lineage and quality monitoring. |
| Data Ownership & Governance | Ownership and stewardship are unclear across business units. | High | Assign Data Owners and Data Stewards for key business domains. |
| Enterprise AI Platform & Architecture | No agreed target architecture for AI and data. | High | Define target architecture for integration, platform, security and deployment. |
| AI Governance, Risk & Operating Model | Policies and lifecycle governance are immature. | Medium | Develop baseline AI governance policies and an AI lifecycle from idea to monitoring. |

## 4. Enterprise AI Readiness Journey

1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 → 6 → 7 → 8

Business Vision → Prioritize Use Cases → Data Inventory → Use-Case Data Readiness → Data Governance → AI Platform & Architecture → AI Deployment & Integration → Continuous Governance

## 5. Executive Takeaways

- Business outcomes should drive AI investments.
- AI-ready data is use-case specific, not universally available.
- Trusted data is the foundation of trustworthy AI.
- Governance should accelerate AI adoption by improving confidence and reducing risk.
